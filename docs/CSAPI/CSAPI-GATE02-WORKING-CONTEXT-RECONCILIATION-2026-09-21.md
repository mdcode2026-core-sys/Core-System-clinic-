Core product principle\nCORE SYSTEM may be highly complex behind the scenes—backend, data relationships, authorization, automation and cross-domain integration—while the frontend must remain extremely simple and understandable.\n\n> Complex behind the scenes; simple in the hands of the user.\n\nThis means architectural complexity must be absorbed by the system, not exposed through unnecessary user decisions or complicated UI. Gate 01 boundary inherited unchanged\nCanonical in-clinic lifecycle:\nReception → Waiting → Clinical Pull → Clinical Work → Finish → Pending Close → Reception Completion → Completed\n\n- Waiting is waiting; it is not clinical start and not role routing.\n- Reception opens/initializes the operational visit and controls queue order/movement.\n- The responsible clinical actor pulls the waiting patient to start clinical work.\n- Clinical completion is named Finish.\n- Finish creates pending close.\n- Reception performs final operational completion.\n- Gate 02 must not reopen or redefine these semantics.\n- Completed means the current Patient Flow/visit operational cycle is complete. It does not mean Patient Journey or the patient relationship has ended.\n\n## 4. Patient Journey is longitudinal, not finite\nPatient Journey is continuous over time. There is no universal Patient Journey Completed, Journey Closed, or Patient Relationship Ended state.\n\nA patient may have no immediate next appointment/action while the longitudinal relationship remains intact. The special terminal case, if any, is intentionally not defined here and remains for later Product Owner clarification.\n\nGate 02's chain is an integration reference, not a universal state machine:\nPatient Identity → Service/Procedure → Booking → Capacity/Availability → Confirmation → Arrival → Patient Flow/Visit → Clinical Decision → Treatment Plan → Next Action → Appointment / Operational Work / Follow-up → Procedure/Session → Financial / Insurance / Resource effects → Next Journey Action → Continuation\n\n## 5. Clinical Recommendations and patient decision\nAfter Clinical Work is finished, the Clinical Role may produce multiple clinical recommendations/needed next procedures. Preferred simple label: Clinical Recommendations.\n\nThe Clinical Role recommends and documents medical needs; it does not command execution.\n\nPer recommendation:\nRecommended → Patient Decision → Accepted / Declined / Deferred → Executed\n\n- The patient decision is central; the clinic cannot force execution.\n- The full medical recommendation remains documented even when declined.\n- Partial acceptance is valid.\n- Complete rejection of a treatment plan is valid.\n- Accepted execution scope may be smaller than the medical recommendation.\n\nExample: a clinician recommends 6 laser sessions and the patient accepts 2. The full medical recommendation remains documented; the accepted execution scope is 2 sessions.\n\n## 6. Patient decision history\nPatient decisions must remain historically traceable.\n\n- If the same decision context remains valid, a prior decision/context may be reopened or reused.\n- If the context changed, a new decision is created.\n\nExamples of changed context include changing from 6 sessions to 4, or requesting the same 6 sessions after the previous commercial offer expired. This is administrative/commercial decision history after the medical recommendation, not a new medical recommendation.\n\n## 7. Continuity is fan-out, not one Next Action\nA completed interaction may produce zero, one, or many downstream consequences. The architecture must not force continuity into one scalar Next Action.\n\nPossible consequences include Treatment Plan, appointment/future visit, Follow-up, Review, prescription, instructions, laboratory/investigation, Operational Work, Communication, other domain-owned consequences, or no immediate action.\n\nEach consequence remains owned by its proper module/domain:\n- Appointment truth → Agenda.\n- Operational Work → Coordination.\n- Communications → Communications.\n- Follow-up → Follow-up.\n- Treatment Plan → its clinical/planning authority.\n- Patient Journey coordinates longitudinal continuity and does not become a second engine.\n\n## 8. Appointment versus continuity checkpoint\nWhen a future visit is known or required, it should be booked before the patient leaves when practical.\nWhen no date/visit is known, the system must not manufacture a fake appointment merely to represent continuity. A Follow-up/continuity checkpoint can preserve the intended next contact.\n\nKnown future visit → Agenda appointment.\nNot yet dateable/visit-ready → appropriate continuity/follow-up mechanism.\nNo current action → continuity still exists.\n\n## 9.
