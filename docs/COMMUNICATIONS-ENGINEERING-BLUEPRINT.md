# CORE SYSTEM — Communications Engineering Blueprint

**Status:** Final pre-implementation engineering reference
**Domain:** Communications
**Scope:** Tenant / clinic operating environment, with Patient Portal communication as a patient-facing channel and future Platform Governance access explicitly separated
**Authority:** This document governs Communications unless a later explicit architectural decision supersedes it.

---

## 1. Purpose

Communications is the communication and coordination layer of CORE SYSTEM. It enables the clinic team to communicate with one another, enables the clinic to communicate with patients through the Patient Portal and supported external channels, and provides system-generated notifications triggered by operational events.

Communications is **not** the owner of Agenda, Clinical, Financial, Inventory, Workforce, Follow-up, or other business domains. It communicates and coordinates around those domains while those domains retain ownership of their business rules and records.

The governing product principle is:

> **Keep communication extremely simple for the user while making the underlying communication, routing, context, audit, delivery, and workflow capability substantially richer.**

## 2. Architectural position

```text
                 CORE SYSTEM
                      │
          ┌───────────┴───────────┐
          │                       │
 Internal Clinic Communication   Patient Communication
          │                       │
          └───────────┬───────────┘
                      │
                Communications
                      │
                Notifications
                      │
        ┌─────────────┼─────────────┐
        ↓             ↓             ↓
      Agenda       Clinical       Financial
        ↓             ↓             ↓
     Inventory     Workforce     Follow-up
                      │
                      ↓
                Operational Data
                      │
                   Insights
                      │
                Future AI layer
```

Communications is a cross-domain service layer. It may reference a business object or create a communication request concerning that object, but it must not duplicate the owning domain's business logic.

---

## 3. Three communication paths

The domain is intentionally divided into three related but distinct paths.

### 3.1 Internal Communication — Core

Communication between users inside the tenant clinic.

Examples:

- doctor ↔ doctor
- doctor ↔ administrative user
- clinical ↔ operational user
- reception ↔ billing
- staff request to inventory
- administrative note to another team member

Internal Communication exists to keep operational work inside CORE instead of forcing the team to rely on verbal requests, WhatsApp, SMS, or other external channels for work that should be traceable.

### 3.2 Patient Communication — Core

Communication between a patient and the clinic through the Patient Portal and, where enabled, external delivery channels.

Examples:

- appointment confirmation
- patient question
- treatment-plan communication
- document request
- payment communication
- follow-up communication
- request for patient action

Patient Portal messaging is a **Core capability**, not merely an advanced add-on, because it is part of the ongoing relationship between the patient and the clinic.

### 3.3 System Notifications — Core infrastructure

Event-driven notifications generated by CORE.

Examples:

- appointment created/changed/cancelled
- payment received or due
- follow-up due
- request assigned
- request completed
- patient action required

Notifications are a delivery mechanism and orchestration layer, not a separate business domain.

---

## 4. Internal Communication model

Internal Communication must distinguish between a normal conversation and an operational request.

### Conversation

A human exchange that does not necessarily require a tracked business action.

### Operational Request

A structured request that asks another user or team to perform an action or provide information.

```text
Requester
   ↓
Operational Request
   ↓
Authorized recipient / responsible team
   ↓
Accept / Execute / Reject / Return
   ↓
Outcome
```

An operational request should be able to retain, where applicable:

- requester
- recipient / responsible user or team
- tenant
- patient context when relevant
- related domain object when relevant
- request category
- priority
- created time
- due time when applicable
- status
- response / outcome
- completion time
- audit information

This model is consistent with the general workflow distinction represented by HL7 FHIR `CommunicationRequest`: a communication request records a request to convey information or initiate communication, while the actual communication is a separate event. CORE should use the concept without becoming FHIR-dependent. citeturn0search1turn0search5

## 5. Permission-aware requests

A user who lacks permission to perform an action must not be forced into a dead end when the business process allows delegation.

Example:

```text
Reception user
   ↓
Needs an inventory action
   ↓
Does not have inventory.execute
   ↓
Can request execution from an authorized user
   ↓
Authorized user performs the action
```

The request does **not** grant the requester the missing permission. The target user's existing effective permissions remain the authority for execution.

This preserves the independent Role / Permission architecture established in Team & Access while allowing work to continue.

Communications therefore provides:

> **communication without permission escalation.**

---

## 6. Communications does not become a Task engine

A communication may create or reference an operational request, and an operational request may lead to a task/work item in the owning domain, but Communications must not become the universal task manager.

```text
Message
  ↓
may create
  ↓
Request
  ↓
may create / trigger
  ↓
Domain-owned work
```

The owning domain remains responsible for execution.

This boundary is important because FHIR similarly distinguishes communication requests from workflow execution resources such as Task. citeturn0search8

---

## 7. Context-linked communication — Core

A communication should be able to reference the business context that caused or explains it.

Examples:

```text
Message → Appointment
Message → Treatment Plan
Message → Invoice
Message → Payment
Message → Follow-up
Message → Document
Message → Inventory request
Message → Clinical encounter
```

The communication layer stores the relationship; it does not take ownership of the referenced object.

This provides the key CORE principle:

> **A message should be meaningful in context, not an isolated piece of text.**

The FHIR Communication model similarly supports contextual references and explicitly separates communication from the request that may have caused it. citeturn0search5turn0search7

---

## 8. Patient Portal communication — Core

Patient Portal is not a separate communication system. It is the patient-facing channel of the Communications domain.

The patient should have one secure communication space for each clinic relationship.

```text
Patient Identity
      ↓
Clinic Relationship
      ↓
Active Clinic
      ↓
Patient Communication
      ├── Messages
      ├── Requests
      ├── Attachments
      ├── Notifications
      └── Required Actions
```

The patient should not need to understand which internal employee handles a message unless the clinic chooses to expose that information.

The clinic, however, must retain routing, ownership, permissions, and audit information internally.

---

## 9. Patient Portal communication context

Patient communication should be linkable to the patient's clinic relationship and, when appropriate, to:

- appointment
- treatment plan
- follow-up
- document
- financial item
- clinical context
- patient action

This creates a continuous relationship rather than a collection of disconnected SMS/WhatsApp conversations.

The intended patient experience is:

> **What does the clinic need from me, and what do I need from the clinic?**

rather than simply:

> **Here is a chat inbox.**

---

## 10. Patient-generated information and review gate

Information submitted by a patient through the Portal must not automatically become authoritative clinical or operational data.

```text
Patient submission
      ↓
Submitted
      ↓
Review when required
      ↓
Accepted / rejected / returned
      ↓
Owning domain record
```

This applies particularly to:

- uploaded medical documents
- patient-provided clinical information
- images where clinical review is required
- information intended for the medical record

Communications transports and records the submission; the owning Clinical or Document domain decides what becomes an authoritative record.

---

## 11. Attachments — Core, richer capability later

Core communication supports appropriate secure attachments, including documents and images where the relevant tenant capability permits them.

Advanced communication may later support richer media such as voice notes or other formats.

Attachments must inherit the same tenant, patient-context, permission, and audit boundaries as the communication that contains them.

---

## 12. Notifications and event-driven communication

CORE should use a common event-to-communication model.

```text
Domain Event
     ↓
Communication Trigger
     ↓
Template / Content
     ↓
Recipient resolution
     ↓
Channel selection
     ↓
Delivery
     ↓
Delivery status / fallback
```

Examples:

```text
Appointment Confirmed
        ↓
Patient notification
```

```text
Follow-up Due
        ↓
Patient notification
```

```text
Internal Request Assigned
        ↓
Staff notification
```

The event originates in the owning domain. Communications reacts to it.

Communications must not recreate the event's business logic.

---

## 13. Channels

The architecture must support:

- In-app / Patient Portal
- in-system internal notifications
- Email
- SMS
- WhatsApp where configured
- future additional channels

The channel is not the business event.

```text
Appointment confirmed
       ↓
Communication
       ↓
Preferred channel
       ↓
Delivery
```

The same event may use different channels according to clinic and recipient preferences.

---

## 14. Communication preferences — Core

Preferences should be simple for ordinary users.

The system should provide sensible defaults and allow controlled overrides.

Conceptually:

```text
Event
  ↓
Default communication policy
  ↓
Recipient preference
  ↓
Allowed clinic channels
  ↓
Selected channel
```

Examples:

- appointment reminder → Portal / WhatsApp
- financial notice → Portal / Email
- internal request → in-system notification

The system should avoid forcing users to configure every event individually.

---

## 15. Templates — Core infrastructure

Templates provide consistent communication while allowing contextual variables.

Examples:

```text
{patient_name}
{clinic_name}
{provider_name}
{appointment_date}
{appointment_time}
{invoice_number}
{amount_due}
```

Templates should support:

- clinic defaults
- language / locale
- event type
- channel compatibility
- variables
- active/inactive status
- version/history where needed

The user-facing editor should remain simple.

---

## 16. Routing

Routing should support both direct and role/team-based recipients.

Examples:

```text
Patient → Clinic
Patient → assigned team
Doctor → Reception
Doctor → Inventory team
Reception → Billing
Admin → specific user
```

Routing must respect Team & Access permissions and must not expose users or conversations outside their effective scope.

Future advanced routing may use workload, skills/capabilities, availability, or assignment rules, but these should not be required for the initial Core implementation.

---

## 17. Internal notes

Internal notes are never visible to the patient.

They may be attached to a relevant internal conversation or operational context.

Example:

```text
Patient conversation
   ├── Patient-visible message
   └── Internal note
```

Internal notes must follow the same authorization and audit principles as other tenant data.

---

## 18. Urgency and safety boundary

Communications is not an emergency-care system.

Patient-facing messaging must clearly communicate that asynchronous messaging is not intended for emergencies or urgent care unless a future explicitly designed service changes this boundary.

Urgency levels for internal operational requests may be supported, but urgency must not override permissions or silently create clinical decisions.

FHIR's CommunicationRequest model similarly provides priority concepts such as routine, urgent, and stat; CORE should use a simple operational model rather than expose clinical interoperability terminology to ordinary users. citeturn0search1

---

## 19. Audit and accountability — Core

Communications must preserve sufficient history to answer:

- who sent it?
- who received it?
- when?
- what was the context?
- what was requested?
- was it delivered/read where applicable?
- was action requested?
- was the request completed?
- who completed it?
- when was it completed?

This is particularly important for internal operational requests because communication becomes part of the evidence of how work was coordinated.

Audit is not intended to create a surveillance product. Its purpose is operational accountability, safety, troubleshooting, and performance analysis.

---

## 20. Relationship to Patient Journey

Communications is **not** a Patient Journey domain.

The relationship is enabling:

```text
Patient Journey / Clinic Operation
          ↓
Needs communication
          ↓
Communications
          ↓
Person / Team / Patient acts
          ↓
Journey / Operation continues
```

Communications must therefore support PJ without duplicating PJ rules.

Examples:

- appointment confirmation supports Agenda
- treatment-plan communication supports Clinical / Treatment Plan
- follow-up message supports Follow-up
- payment message supports Financial
- preparation request supports Journey Coordination / Operations

---

## 21. Relationship to Agenda

Agenda remains the owner of appointments, availability, booking, conflicts, and scheduling rules.

Communications receives relevant events and sends appropriate messages.

```text
Agenda
  ↓
Appointment confirmed
  ↓
Communications
  ↓
Patient / staff notification
```

Communications must not create a second appointment engine.

---

## 22. Relationship to Financial & Resources

Financial & Resources remains responsible for invoices, payments, installments, purchasing, inventory, suppliers, consumption, and financial rules.

Communications may:

- notify about invoices
- notify about payments
- request a financial action
- send a patient financial message
- route an internal request to an authorized financial user

It must not calculate financial balances independently.

---

## 23. Relationship to Workforce & Operations

Workforce & Operations remains responsible for staff, availability, scheduling/capacity, productivity, and operational performance.

Communications may:

- route requests
- notify staff
- support handoffs
- record operational coordination
- provide communication history for later analysis

It must not become the workforce scheduler or HR engine.

---

## 24. Relationship to Team & Access

Team & Access remains the authority for:

- users
- roles
- permissions
- permission catalog
- permission sets/bundles
- overrides
- audit
- user settings

Communications consumes effective permissions.

Roles remain independent from permissions.

A request can be sent to someone who has the required permission, but the request itself does not grant that permission.

Workspace remains a UX organization mechanism, not a replacement for authorization.

---

## 25. Relationship to Insights

Insights consumes communication data; Communications does not own analytical interpretation.

Useful future measurements include:

- response time
- request completion time
- overdue requests
- communication volume
- delivery success
- patient response rates
- follow-up response
- communication-related operational delays

These measurements can help explain Patient Journey outcomes without making Communications responsible for them.

---

## 26. Relationship to Patient Portal

Patient Portal is a patient-facing application surface. Communications is the communication service behind it.

The Portal should not implement its own independent messaging engine.

```text
Patient Portal UI
       ↓
Communications capability
       ↓
Tenant-scoped conversation / request
       ↓
Owning domain when action is required
```

This keeps the patient experience unified while preserving domain ownership.

---

## 27. Subscription / entitlement principle

Communication architecture and historical operational data should not be unnecessarily destroyed or disabled merely because a capability is not exposed in a tenant's current subscription.

Conceptually:

```text
Operational Event
      ↓
Communication capability
      ↓
Tenant entitlement + user permission
      ↓
Visible / actionable capability
```

Basic and Advanced packages may expose different communication capabilities, but the underlying event and data foundation should be designed for future expansion.

Patient Portal messaging is considered Core. Advanced channels, richer routing, advanced automation, and richer media may be separately packaged.

---

## 28. Platform Governance / Super Admin boundary

Super Admin is outside the tenant operating environment.

Super Admin must not appear in the tenant sidebar or tenant Team & Access model as an operational user.

Future Platform Governance may have platform-level access to communication-related aggregate or final analytical outputs where appropriate for support, training, coaching, product improvement, or customer success.

This does **not** authorize unrestricted access to:

- patient conversations
- raw clinical content
- staff conversations
- tenant private messages
- raw attachments
- patient personal data

The exact Platform Governance boundary is a future domain decision.

---

## 29. Privacy and tenant isolation

Every communication query, mutation, attachment, notification, and request must be tenant-scoped server-side.

Patient Portal access must additionally enforce the patient's clinic relationship and the scope of information that the clinic has authorized for patient access.

The UI must never be the sole security boundary.

Cross-tenant access must be impossible through manipulated identifiers, routes, API requests, or client state.

---

## 30. AI readiness

Communications must be structured from day one for future AI use without requiring AI to be part of the first implementation.

Useful future signals include:

```text
Who communicated?
To whom?
About what?
Why?
When?
What was requested?
Was it answered?
Was action taken?
How long did it take?
What happened afterwards?
```

This creates a high-value historical dataset for future agents that may assist with:

- routing
- summarization
- identifying overdue requests
- detecting operational bottlenecks
- preparing patient communications
- identifying patient actions still pending
- recommending next actions under controlled permissions

AI must not independently send clinical or operational actions merely because it can infer them. Future AI actions remain permission- and workflow-controlled.

## 32. UX principles

1. One obvious Communications entry point where appropriate.
2. Separate patient-facing and internal communication views.
3. Do not expose technical communication terminology to ordinary users.
4. Make requests look different from ordinary chat.
5. Show the next action clearly.
6. Keep status understandable: Open / Waiting / Done / Closed where sufficient.
7. Progressive disclosure: detail on demand.
8. Avoid Slack/WhatsApp-style complexity.
9. Do not force users to learn routing rules.
10. Respect existing RTL/LTR and language foundations.
11. Mobile use must remain practical for staff and patients.

---

## 33. Explicitly rejected directions

- Building Slack/Teams as a separate product inside CORE.
- Building a generic social chat platform.
- Making Communications the owner of tasks.
- Making Communications the owner of Agenda, Financial, Inventory, Clinical, Workforce, or Follow-up logic.
- Treating every message as a task.
- Granting permissions through a communication request.
- Automatically promoting patient-submitted information to authoritative clinical data.
- Making external channels such as WhatsApp the system of record.
- Making Patient Portal messaging an optional cosmetic feature.
- Adding Super Admin as a tenant operational role.
- Building AI before the underlying communication data and workflow model is reliable.

---

## 34. Final reconciliation with approved CORE architecture

Communications is consistent with the established architecture when the following boundaries are preserved:

| Area | Owner | Communications role |
|---|---|---|
| Patient Journey | PJ / Clinical domains | Enable communication around journey events |
| Agenda | Agenda | Notify / request / communicate |
| Financial & Resources | Financial domain | Notify / request / communicate |
| Workforce & Operations | Workforce domain | Route / notify / coordinate |
| Team & Access | Team & Access | Enforce effective permissions |
| Patient Portal | Patient Portal surface | Provide communication capability |
| Insights | Insights | Consume communication data |
| Platform Governance | Future separate platform | Future controlled analytical/support access |

The resulting model is:

```text
                    COMMUNICATIONS
                           │
          ┌────────────────┼────────────────┐
          │                │                │
       Internal          Patient        Notifications
      Communication    Communication
          │                │                │
          ↓                ↓                ↓
       Clinic          Portal /         Domain
        Team           Patient          Events
          │                │                │
          └────────────────┼────────────────┘
                           ↓
                    CORE operational flow
                           ↓
                       Insights
                           ↓
                     Future AI
```

## 35. Implementation-phase requirements

The engineering implementation must:

1. Reconcile the current Communications and Patient Portal code with this blueprint before changing behavior.
2. Reuse existing tenant/auth/permission infrastructure.
3. Reuse existing Patient Portal identity and clinic relationship architecture rather than creating a second patient identity.
4. Establish a clear distinction between Conversation, Operational Request, Notification, and Delivery Event.
5. Establish context references without duplicating owning-domain records.
6. Enforce tenant and patient-clinic isolation server-side.
7. Implement internal requests without permission escalation.
8. Implement Patient Portal messaging as a first-class Core capability.
9. Implement event-triggered notifications using owning-domain events.
10. Preserve channel abstraction so Portal, Email, SMS, WhatsApp, and future channels are delivery mechanisms rather than separate business workflows.
11. Implement templates and communication preferences with simple UX.
12. Preserve auditability and useful delivery/request lifecycle states.
13. Ensure patient-generated submissions can pass through a review gate where required.
14. Prepare structured communication data for Insights and future AI.
15. Do not introduce a second task engine, authorization system, appointment engine, financial engine, or clinical record system.

These are implementation requirements, not permission to reopen the architectural decisions above.

---

## 36. Definition of architectural completion

The Communications domain is architecturally complete when:

- internal clinic communication works without requiring external messaging for ordinary operational coordination;
- operational requests can be routed to authorized users without granting permissions;
- patient communication is available through the Patient Portal as a first-class capability;
- messages can be meaningfully linked to relevant clinic operations;
- notifications are event-driven and channel-independent;
- templates and preferences remain simple to operate;
- audit and delivery/request lifecycle information are retained;
- tenant and patient relationship isolation is enforced server-side;
- Communications does not duplicate owning-domain business logic;
- Insights can consume structured communication outcomes;
- the data foundation is suitable for future AI assistance;
- the user experience remains substantially simpler than the underlying architecture.

---

## 37. Governing principle

> **CORE Communications exists to keep the clinic's work and the patient's relationship with the clinic inside the operational context of CORE — not to build another chat application.**

The success criterion is therefore not the number of messaging features. It is whether communication makes the clinic easier to operate, makes requests traceable without becoming bureaucratic, protects permissions without blocking work, and makes the patient's relationship with the clinic continuous, organized, and useful throughout the Patient Journey.
